Quantitative Genetics Reference

Core Equations & Experimental Design Guide

1. Partitioning Phenotypic Value & Variance

Every individual's observable trait value P is decomposed into genetic and environmental deviations around the population mean μ:

P = μ + A + D + E + (G × E)
VP = VA + VD + VE + VG×E = VG + VE + VG×E

2. Broad-Sense vs. Narrow-Sense Heritability

Broad-sense heritability (H²) measures the proportion of total phenotypic variance caused by all genetic factors (VG = VA + VD). Narrow-sense heritability (h²) measures the proportion caused solely by additive breeding values (VA), which govern parent-offspring resemblance and response to selection:

H² = VG / VP = (VA + VD) / VP
h² = VA / VP = Cov(POffspring, PMid-Parent) / Var(PMid-Parent)

3. The Breeder's Equation

When truncation selection shifts the mean of selected parents by a selection differential S = Z̄S − Z̄0, the evolutionary response in the next generation R = Z̄1 − Z̄0 equals:

R = h² × S   ⇒   h²realized = R / S

4. Genotype-by-Environment Interaction (G × E)

When full-sibling families are reared across two contrasting environments (e.g., Lush Meadow vs. Arid Scrub), plotting family means across habitats reveals reaction norms. Parallel lines indicate VG×E = 0, whereas non-parallel or crossing slopes indicate significant VG×E > 0.
